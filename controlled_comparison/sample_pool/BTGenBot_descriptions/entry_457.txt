The behavior tree represents a decision-making process for an autonomous vehicle. The vehicle is programmed to navigate through traffic while adhering to various conditions and actions. It begins by checking if there is a need to wait for a mission, and if not, it stops. If there's no path available globally, it checks the lane-keeping state and potential lane changes, then initializes the path. Subsequently, it handles lane keeping, lane changes, traffic light conditions, unprotected turns, and mission states. Throughout these sequences, it continuously evaluates various conditions such as collision detection, proximity to objects, irregularities, and lane change feasibility.

The tree employs a range of actions including generating paths, setting states, calling global paths, handling traffic lights, publishing data, and stopping. Additionally, it utilizes numerous conditions to assess the vehicle's surroundings and make decisions accordingly. Overall, the behavior tree orchestrates the vehicle's navigation, ensuring it follows safety protocols, navigates efficiently, and responds appropriately to its environment.
